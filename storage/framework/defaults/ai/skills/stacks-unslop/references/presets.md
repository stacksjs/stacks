# Voice presets

Adapted from Unslop's crisp, warm, expert, and story presets. Read only the
chosen section. Use the source register when none is requested.
[core-contract.md](core-contract.md) decides whether an edit is allowed and
what it must preserve. A preset never supplies facts, anecdotes, opinions,
certainty, or permission to restructure otherwise unchanged prose.

## Crisp

Prefer direct, economical sentences and plain verbs. Keep concrete details the
source supplies. Remove a formulaic opener only when it contributes nothing.
Keep intentional hedges, natural paragraphing, and technical language. Do not
impose a sentence-length target, active voice, or short paragraphs on prose
that already works. Avoid turning concision into telegraphic fragments.

## Warm

Write as to a respected colleague. Use contractions and reader address when
they fit the source. Keep a natural transition and a useful next step when
the original contains one. Avoid forced intimacy, manufactured enthusiasm,
and generic reassurance. Warmth cannot add a promise or an anecdote.

## Expert

Use precise terminology, a clear assertion, and the evidence the source gives.
Keep limits and uncertainty as carefully as the claim. Demonstrate expertise
through specificity rather than credentials or confident phrasing. Do not
turn "might work" into "works" or create a number to sound authoritative.

## Story

Let supplied events, observations, and dialogue carry the point. Preserve
chronology, attribution, and tense when a change would alter meaning. Give a
real scene enough room without adding sensory details or invented quotations.
End on a supplied event or observation when an empty moral merely repeats it.
Preserve unique information in the ending, even if its placement is awkward.
