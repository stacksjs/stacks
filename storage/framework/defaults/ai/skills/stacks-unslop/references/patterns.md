# Contextual pattern catalog

Adapted from Unslop's phrase, structure, and discourse catalogs. Read the
sections relevant to the source. This catalog suggests candidates;
[core-contract.md](core-contract.md) decides whether an edit is justified.
Examples describe patterns, not banned words. Do not alter a quoted example,
proper name, API identifier, literal term, or a genre convention on a match.

## Openings and emphasis

| Candidate | Defect to confirm | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "Let me be clear" | A staged opener delays a routine point. | Start with the point. |
| "Full stop", "Let that sink in", "Read that again" | A closer asks for emphasis without adding information. | Remove the closer. |
| "The real question", "At its core" | An ordinary claim is dressed as a revelation. | State the specific claim. |
| "The stakes are real", "This matters because" | Importance is announced rather than explained. | Keep the actual consequence. |
| "What does this mean for you?", "What's next?" | A question introduces an answer the reader did not ask for. | State the answer when it carries information. |
| "You're asking about", "To answer your question" | The reply repeats the prompt before answering. | Answer directly. |
| "Let me think step by step" | Drafting or reasoning narration remains in finished copy. | Keep the useful explanation. |

Protect a genuine correction, a real question, and an explanation that helps
the intended reader. Repeated scaffolding across paragraphs is stronger
evidence than one ordinary connective.

## Inflation and register

| Candidate | Defect to confirm | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "Game-changer", "best-in-class", "revolutionary" | Praise names no mechanism, measure, or action. | Use the supported effect. |
| "Leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem", "bandwidth" | Jargon hides an ordinary action. | Use a plain equivalent with the same meaning. |
| "Pivotal moment", "lasting legacy", "stands as a testament" | Significance exceeds the source. | Keep the fact. |
| "Delve", "tapestry", "intricate", "multifaceted" | Several stock words inflate simple information. | Use the source's concrete terms. |
| "Nestled", "breathtaking", "hidden gem" | Promotion substitutes for description in neutral prose. | Say what the place or product is. |
| "Highlighting", "showcasing", "underscoring" after a clause | An added interpretation supplies no supported consequence. | Retain the supported clause. |
| "Experts argue", "many believe", "widely regarded" | Authority is borrowed without an identified source. | Keep a real attribution or flag missing support. |
| "Serves as", "constitutes", "functions as" | An inflated construction obscures a simple identity. | Use "is" where the relationship is identical. |
| "Introduced a new concept", "nobody talks about" | Novelty is asserted without support. | Describe the idea itself. |
| "Always", "never", "everyone" | Rhetorical certainty contradicts the source's scope. | Restore the supported scope. |

Protect literal network bandwidth, an ecosystem in biology, a documented
turning point, a named expert's claim, and mandatory words in specifications.
Do not weaken a precise rule to improve its tone. Formal vocabulary and
promotional register can be intentional; inspect their function in context.

## Sentence framing

| Candidate | Defect to confirm | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "Not X but Y", including a two-sentence contrast | The rejected half answers an objection nobody raised. | State Y directly. |
| "While challenges remain, the future is bright" | A concession and reassurance supply no concrete information. | Keep actual challenges and plans. |
| "Could potentially possibly" | Qualifiers accumulate without adding distinct uncertainty. | Keep the qualifier the source needs. |
| Repeated parenthetical hedges | Every sentence stages caution instead of stating its scope. | Preserve real uncertainty in the claim. |
| "From X to Y, from A to B" | A sweeping range equates unrelated categories. | Name the supported items without a false scale. |
| One entity cycles through "company", "firm", "enterprise" | Synonym rotation makes identity harder to follow. | Use a stable name. |
| "The tool wants", "the suite refuses" | Personification hides an actor or actual behavior. | State the operation or result. |
| A sudden aphorism or mixed metaphor | Figurative language contradicts itself or adds empty abstraction. | State the supported relationship. |

Keep genuine contrasts, opposing views, metaphors the writer uses meaningfully,
and a literal technical use such as "the server refuses a connection". A
wording preference alone is insufficient to confirm a defect.

## Formatting and leftovers

- A numbered list promises an arbitrary number of takeaways without distinct
  items. Keep the count the content needs, preserving all unique claims.
- Every line begins with a bold label and repeats it in the sentence. Repair
  the duplication; keep lookup lists in reference docs when they aid scanning.
- Title Case and decorative emojis impose a template on ordinary body copy.
  Follow the document's actual heading and capitalization conventions.
- Repeated emphatic punctuation supplies energy instead of content. Use
  sentence punctuation appropriate to the register and Stacks' copy rules.
- "Great question", "I hope this helps", or an unsolicited continuation offer
  remains around a standalone article. Remove the chat wrapper. Keep a real
  letter's salutation or requested call to action.
- A knowledge-cutoff disclaimer becomes a guess about an undocumented fact.
  Retain accurate uncertainty; remove a guess rather than manufacture evidence.
- "This section explores" describes the document where the reader needs its
  subject. Keep procedural framing only when it tells the reader how to act.

## Structure and discourse

Inspect paragraphs and the arrangement of ideas, not only phrases:

- **Uniform rhythm:** every sentence has the same length or opening. Confirm
  when it makes the explanation harder to follow; preserve an intentional
  refrain. Do not impose a numeric sentence-length target.
- **Staccato repair:** the rewrite becomes a run of fragments. Reconnect the
  clauses according to their meaning rather than manufacture punch lines.
- **Forced triads:** three parallel examples repeat one point. Preserve three
  real facts; cut only empty repetition.
- **Connective scaffolding:** every paragraph starts "However", "Moreover",
  or "Additionally" instead of its own claim. Keep a connective that explains
  a real relationship.
- **Preview and recap loops:** the introduction previews an outline, the body
  follows it mechanically, and the ending repeats it. Keep genuine abstracts,
  executive summaries, and instructional roadmaps when they serve the reader.
- **Rotating paragraph roles:** example, lesson, reassurance, and transition
  repeat as a template. Confirm that the repeated roles add no new information
  before changing the structure.
- **Moralizing coda:** an ending tells the reader what an example already
  showed. Keep an ending that adds a real consequence or action.
- **Templated redemption arc:** every account follows failure, lesson,
  transformation, and uplift. Respect an actual memoir or case study's events.
- **Uniform confidence or balance:** every paragraph ends with polished
  reassurance or both-sides framing. Preserve neutrality in reference prose
  and genuine unresolved uncertainty.

Removing a discourse cue does not repair a repetitive argument. Check whether
the resulting paragraph actually contributes a distinct claim. Macro edits
require scope from the user; minimal cleanup preserves unaffected paragraphs.
